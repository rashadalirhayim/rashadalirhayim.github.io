One of the first things you might consider when applying to graduate schools is writing the Statement of Purpose (SoP) or Personal Statement. This is your chance to help the admissions committee understand your purpose in pursuing graduate school and why you are applying to their specific program.

If you know you want to pursue graduate school but don't know where to start the writing process, follow these simple steps.

---

## 1. Give yourself ample time
You don’t have to be a "good writer" to write a good personal statement, but you certainly need to make an effort. Effort takes time. If you procrastinate, you won't have the space to develop your statement as you learn more about your aspirations.

## 2. List the Essay Prompts
Make a list of all the statement prompts for the programs you wish to apply to. This helps you understand the scope (word limits, focus) and visualize the requirements of each program.

![ ](/images/sop-requirements-table.jpg)

## 3. Make an Outline
An outline prevents your essay from looking like a patched-up piece of work. Most programs want you to discuss:
* Reasons for pursuing graduate studies
* Research topics you would like to explore 
* Why you are interested in their specific program
* Academic and professional goals

---

## Structure Matters: Paragraph by Paragraph

### A. The First Paragraph: Introductory Narrative
Show the committee you are invested. Mention a particular moment or obstacle that inspired you. Be specific—that is how you stay memorable.

> **Goal:** Show enthusiasm and that you have thought carefully about this path.

![ ](/images/intro-sample-1.jpg)

*Commentary:* This introduction is alright, but it could be stronger by focusing on THE moment interests in the social aspects of architecture started.

![ ](/images/intro-sample-2.jpg)

*Common Mistake:* Don't treat your statement as a factual, chronological list of experiences. **Focus only on pivotal moments.**

### B. The Second Paragraph: Ideas for Future Research
PhD programs want applicants who know what problems they want to focus on and which professors they want to work with.

![ ](/images/research-ideas-sample.jpg)

### C. The Third Paragraph: Why THIS Program?
Show them how *their* program is the solution to your research problem. Mention:
* Specific classes or labs.
* Faculty members you want to work with.
* Relevant internships or facilities.

![ ](/images/why-program-sample.jpg)

### D. The Last Paragraph: Academic and Professional Goals
Wrap up by showing where this degree will take you in the long term.

![ ](/images/goals-sample.jpg)

---

## 4. Ask for Feedback
Show your final draft to faculty members, fellow students, or mentors. I also highly recommend the **r/gradadmissions** community on Reddit for peer support.

## 5. Final Thoughts
You aren't just trying to show you are smart; you are showing you want to build on existing knowledge. 

* **Balance** experience with a desire to learn.
* **Show... Don't Tell!**
