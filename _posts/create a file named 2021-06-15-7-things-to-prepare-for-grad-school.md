Your excitement can quickly transform to anxiety once you watch the weeks pass by quickly and realize you are closely approaching your first day as a PhD student. But it doesn’t have to be this way. If you plan well and prepare for grad school, you will find the confidence to become focused and excited again.

This is why I believe the months between accepting a PhD and starting one are really crucial and you should use wisely. In this blog I will discuss some of the things I started to do over the past few months to prepare myself for my PhD journey. 

---

## 1. Read the student handbook
The student handbook is a document that’s usually available on a program’s website which lists all the requirements expected for the completion of a PhD. This may involve:
* **Comprehensive examinations** (written and oral)
* **Research and scholarly training** (research apprenticeship and Teaching Fellowships)
* **The dissertation** (proposal and defense)

## 2. Connect with your advisor
I received an invitation from my advisor to meet (on zoom) to discuss next steps. Having read the program’s website and handbook, I already had an idea of the requirements, milestones, and academic placements available to me. However, there are times when people forget to explain minor details—it’s important you prepare yourself with a set of questions.

## 3. Connect with current graduate students
The best way to connect with current graduate students is to ask your advisor to connect you. I wrote an email asking my advisor for advice on neighborhoods to rent in and whether she could connect me to students in the program. 

**Questions to ask Graduate Students?**
Since I have a one-year old daughter, I was interested in connecting with parents:
* Where are the safest neighborhoods to live in?
* What are the best/worst things about living there?
* What daycare options are available that you would recommend?
* How challenging has the PhD been so far while raising a kid?

## 4. Plan your finances
You don’t have to be a finance guru to know that you are going to have to do some serious budgeting.

## 5. Read. EVERYDAY!
It’s extremely important that you familiarize yourself with the most significant discourses and trends in your field. If you don’t, you will have trouble knowing:
* What has already been done in your field.
* The most important questions—so you won’t be able to judge relevance.
* The methods and approaches used.

> ‘This is like becoming fluent in a foreign language: If you’ve never been through the foundations of its grammar, you’ll never be able to speak it without mistakes.’ — *Tress academic*

![ ](/images/reading-books.gif)

## 6. Develop your note-taking skills
Being able to retain and retrieve information is a VERY important skill. Personally, I found the **Zettelkasten** method to be the most effective. 

* **Read:** ‘How to Take Smart Notes’ by Sönke Ahrens.
* **Tool:** Find a tool you’re comfortable with. I currently use ‘Roam Research’ and it has been a game changer.
* **Implement:** There are countless blogs on how to use the Zettelkasten method in Roam.
