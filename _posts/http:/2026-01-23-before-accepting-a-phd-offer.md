Excitement is quickly replaced with anxiety once you realize the weeks are passing by and you are closely approaching your first day as a PhD student. But it doesn’t have to be this way. If you plan ahead and are well prepared (physically and mentally), you will give yourself the confidence that will help you get a strong start. 

That’s why I believe the months between accepting a PhD and starting one are really crucial ones and should be used wisely. In this blog, I will discuss some of the things I started to do over the past few months to prepare myself for my PhD journey. 

> *Note: I may edit this post as I progress in the program to add points that I could have missed out.*

---

## 1. Read the Student Handbook
One of the first things I did after accepting my offer was to read the student handbook. Of course, I read it before applying to the program, but I felt I needed to go over it again more carefully. 

The student handbook is a document that lists all the requirements expected for the completion of a PhD. This may involve:
* **Comprehensive examinations** (written and oral)
* **Research and scholarly training** (research apprenticeship and Teaching Fellowships)
* **The dissertation** (proposal and defense)

It’s important that you read this document carefully to **understand what’s expected from you.** It is also a crucial step in planning and carrying out your graduate study. 

## 2. Connect with Your Advisor
After familiarizing myself with the handbook, I received an invitation from my advisor to meet (via Zoom) to discuss next steps. Having read the program’s website, I already had an idea of the requirements, milestones, and academic placements available to me. I did, however, have some questions about financial opportunities. 

I consider myself lucky because my advisor was thorough and explained everything to the last detail. However, there are times when people forget to explain minor details—it’s important you prepare yourself with a **set of questions** beforehand.

## 3. Connect with Current Graduate Students
The best way to connect with current graduate students is to ask your advisor to introduce you. I wrote an email asking my advisor for advice on neighborhoods to rent in and whether she could connect me to students in the program. I received tons of helpful responses! 

If that’s not an option, you can search the program’s student club directory or social media. 

**Questions to ask Graduate Students?**
Because I have a daughter, I was interested in talking to parents about their experiences. Here are some of the questions I asked:
* Where are the safest neighborhoods to live in?
* What are the best/worst things about living there?
* What daycare options are available that you would recommend? 
* How challenging has the PhD been so far while raising a child?

## 4. Plan Your Finances
You don’t have to be a finance guru to know that you are going to have to do some serious budgeting. I found vlogs on the subject very useful, especially since I am new to planning around a strict graduate budget.

---

## 5. Read every day!
It’s extremely important that you familiarize yourself with the most significant discourses and trends in your field. If you don’t, you will have trouble knowing:
1. What has already been done.
2. The most important questions (so you can judge the relevance of an idea).
3. The methods and approaches commonly used.

> "This is like becoming fluent in a foreign language: If you’ve never been through the foundations of its grammar, you’ll never be able to speak it without mistakes." — *Tress Academic*

### Where do you start?
* **Consult your advisor:** Ask them to refer you to relevant papers and books. 
* **Read the syllabus:** Search Google for the course number and name + "syllabus" (e.g., *CRPLAN 5000 syllabus*). If you can't find it, ask a student who has taken the course.

## 6. Develop Your Note-Taking Skills
Nothing is worse than forgetting the source of something you read months ago. Being able to retain and retrieve information is a **critical skill** for a researcher. When you take smarter notes, you will:
* Retain more information.
* Develop your own unique insights.
* Find connections between complex ideas.

**My Recommendation: The Zettelkasten Method**
Personally, I found the **Zettelkasten** method to be the most effective. 
* **Read:** *How to Take Smart Notes* by Sönke Ahrens. 
* **Tool:** Find a tool you are comfortable with. I currently use **Roam Research** and it has been a game changer. (Students can often apply for a scholarship for a reduced price!)
* **Implementation:** There are countless blogs on how to use Zettelkasten within Roam to help you get started.
