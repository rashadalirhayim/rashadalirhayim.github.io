I’m so glad to share that I finally submitted my first paper for review! It has been almost two years since I wrote my thesis, and it feels great to finally have it out there. While I am nervous about the feedback, I hope the hard work pays off with a publication soon.

So, exactly how do you publish your first paper? While the process differs by field, there are some constants. Your first priority should be converting your thesis into a manuscript. Since my thesis took a year to write, it was the most logical choice for my first submission.

---

## Where should I publish my thesis?

The best way to get recognition is to publish in a **peer-reviewed recognized journal** in your field. Generally, you will choose between two primary models:



| Feature | Open-Access (OA) | Traditional Publishing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accessibility** | Available to everyone digitally for free. | Often requires a subscription or library access. |
| **Speed** | Often features a faster digital turnaround. | Can have longer lead times for print/review. |
| **Editing** | Varies, but often less intensive formatting. | High standards for formatting and professional editing. |
| **Cost** | May involve an Article Processing Charge (APC). | Usually free for authors, but costs the reader. |

### Finding the Right Fit
Find out which journals accept papers similar to your subject. Search for keywords, read abstracts, and—most importantly—**ask your advisor** which journals carry the most weight in your specific field.

---

## The Path to Submission

### 1. Read the Guidelines
Every journal provides "Instructions for Authors." Read these before you even begin editing. They detail the review process, specific formatting styles (APA, Chicago, etc.), and any potential submission fees.

### 2. Edit and Format
Formatting can be overwhelming, but you must meet the standards of your chosen journal exactly. This shows the editors you are serious. Pay close attention to:
* **Word counts**
* **Citation styles**
* **Submission deadlines**

### 3. Proofread Before Submitting
A paper full of typos won't be taken seriously by reviewers. Careless mistakes detract from the scholarly research process and may prevent you from receiving constructive feedback. 

> **Tip:** Ask the editor about their typical response time. The earlier you start your revisions, the better prepared you will be for the final submission.

---

## Final Thoughts
Publishing a thesis is a marathon, not a sprint. It is both intimidating and exciting. The best advice I can give is to **start as early as possible** and look for opportunities as they arise. 

**Publish early, publish often!**
