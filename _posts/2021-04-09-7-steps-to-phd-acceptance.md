In mid-January 2021, I received an acceptance letter from **The Ohio State University (OSU)**. After facing rejections the previous year, this moment was a thrilling validation of my journey. 

![My OSU Acceptance Letter](/images/osu-acceptance.jpg)
*That moment of relief: The official offer from Ohio State.*

---

## 1. Make a Plan
Any plan requires both a **timeframe** and **milestones**. If you contact a potential supervisor too close to a deadline, they may sense negligence. 

| Milestone | Recommended Timing |
| :--- | :--- |
| Application Research | 12 Months Out |
| Contact Potential Supervisors | 8–10 Months Out |
| Study for GRE/TOEFL | 6–8 Months Out |
| Draft Statement of Purpose | 4–6 Months Out |
| Submit Applications | 1–2 Months Before Deadline |

## 2. Research Potential Programs
Narrowing your focus is key. It’s not enough to be interested in "Planning." You need to find your specific niche. I used a comparison table to track faculty interests, funding, and location.


## 3. Understand and Strengthen Your Weaknesses
Most programs won't tell you why you were rejected. You have to be your own critic. 
* **The "Pause" Strategy:** After my first round of rejections, I took a year off. I used that time to reflect on *why* I wanted the degree.
* **Skill Building:** If your GRE is low, retake it. If your research experience is thin, look for research assistantships or start a technical blog (like this one!) to demonstrate your thought process.

## 4. Apply to Different "Kinds" of Programs
Don't just apply to the top-ranked schools on Google. I suggest a balanced "Portfolio" approach:
* **Reach Schools (2):** Competitive dream schools (e.g., Harvard, MIT).
* **Match Schools (3):** Where your scores align with their average student profile.
* **Safety Schools (2):** Schools where you are highly likely to be admitted.


## 5. Focus on your Statement of Purpose (SoP)
The SoP is the heart of your application. It’s where you "put your money where it belongs."
* **Don't just list achievements:** That's what the CV is for. Use the SoP to tell a story of *why* your research question matters.
* **Be Specific:** Mention specific faculty members at that school and how your work aligns with theirs.

## 6. Secure Strong Letters of Recommendation (LoRs)
Choose people who know your **potential for research**, not just people who gave you an 'A'. Give your recommenders at least two months' notice and provide them with a summary of your goals so they can write a targeted letter.

## 7. Prepare for the Interview
If you get an invite, you are "admissible" on paper; the interview is about **fit**. Be ready to discuss your past work and your future research interests with confidence.

---

### Final Thoughts: Put your money where it belongs
Don’t be fooled by "guaranteed admission" services. Those who get into top programs usually do the hard work of writing and revising their own narratives. Save your money for the application fees... and for school itself! :)
